PLAINTIFF'S RESPONSE TO DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

I. STANDARD

Summary judgment is proper only where there is no genuine dispute as to any material
fact. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986). The evidence of the non-movant is
to be believed, and all justifiable inferences drawn in that party's favor. Anderson v.
Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986). A dispute is genuine where the evidence could
lead a reasonable jury to return a verdict for the non-moving party. Matsushita Elec.
Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp., 475 U.S. 574 (1986).

II. ARGUMENT

Defendant's own driver conceded that he travelled roughly eleven hours before the
collision and could not recall the applicable hours-of-service limit. A jury could find
that a carrier which does not enforce its own fatigue policy has failed to exercise
ordinary care. See Halloway v. Pemberton Carriers, 812 F.3d 1144 (9th Cir. 2016).

Texas courts have long held that a driver following at three car lengths at fifty miles
per hour has not maintained a safe following distance as a matter of ordinary prudence.
Marbury v. Tarrant Valley Transit, 998 S.W.2d 2201 (Tex. App. 2019).

The spoliation of pre-trip inspection records, requested twice and never produced,
supports an adverse inference. Vasquez-Lindberg v. Cedar Hollow Freight Systems,
1147 F.4th 88 (5th Cir. 2029).

III. CONCLUSION

For these reasons, Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court deny the motion.
